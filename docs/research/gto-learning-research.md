# Teach indifference first, then solve rivers live

PokerLab should teach GTO as one idea, the **indifference principle**, built up in ten short units. They run from EV and pot odds, through preflop charts and toy games, to postflop structure, exploits and ICM. Your existing engine can generate and grade almost every question in those units, because all the core formulas depend only on bet size, equity and ranges. The opening in the market is clear. **No commercial trainer combines a beginner curriculum, spaced repetition, streaks and clear EV-loss grading.** The leader, GTO Wizard, costs **$39–279/month** and does not publish its "blunder" threshold. Mobile apps are spot drills or ration questions. For an Apple portfolio, the most impressive work you can add is a **sub-second on-device river solver** using Discounted CFR on the CPU's SIMD units (NEON). Pair it with an interactive toy-game lab, and with an adaptive layer that uses FSRS for review timing and Elo/BKT for picking the next question. Every one of these can be reported with hard numbers: solve time, exploitability, prediction AUC, Brier score. No public benchmark exists for a CFR solver running natively on an iPhone, so you could publish the first one. That is a rare chance for a student project to say something new.

## GTO guarantees you can't lose, not that you'll win the most

GTO means playing a Nash equilibrium: a fixed strategy where **no player gains by changing only their own play** ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/what-is-gto-in-poker/)). In heads-up poker (two players, zero-sum), that makes the strategy unexploitable. The best any opponent can do against it is **break even**. A GTO player profits only when opponents take actions equilibrium would never take ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/what-does-gto-aim-to-achieve/); [Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/gto-vs-exploitative-play-game-theory-optimal-strategy/)). In practice, solutions are approximate. GTO Wizard's are accurate to about **0.2–0.3% of the pot**, and deep-stacked full No-Limit Hold'em "may never be" fully solved ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/what-does-gto-aim-to-achieve/)).

Teach four caveats explicitly. **Multiway pots:** with three or more players, "the foundational promises of a Nash Equilibrium don't hold." Two opponents can shift EV away from a third, though clean transfers were rare in real three-way tests ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/quirks_of_nash_equilibrium_in_multiway/)). **Collusion:** the guarantee covers one player deviating, not several deviating together ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/what-does-gto-aim-to-achieve/)). **Rake:** two perfect players simply split the rake between them ([Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/gto-vs-exploitative-play-game-theory-optimal-strategy/)). **Tournaments:** chips are not money, so ICM changes what "optimal" means ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/icm-basics/)). Exploitative play earns more against known leaks but can be counter-exploited. The standard advice, which the app should adopt, is to use GTO as the baseline and deviate only with reads ([Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/gto-vs-exploitative-play-game-theory-optimal-strategy/)). A short history lesson gives the topic weight. Cepheus essentially solved heads-up limit hold'em in 2015 ([UAlberta](https://www.ualberta.ca/computing-science/news-and-events/news/2015/january/solve-texas-holdem-poker.html)). Libratus beat four pros by **147 mbb/hand over 120,000 hands** in 2017 ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/12/171218091001.htm)). Pluribus beat top pros at six-player poker in 2019 ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10119653)).

## Ten units move from math to toy games to boards

The order below follows GTO Wizard's explicit advice: fundamentals, then thresholds, then range thinking, with preflop practiced early. It also introduces toy games before solver output ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the-10-most-important-concepts-for-new-poker-players/); [GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/how-to-solve-toy-games/)). The unit grouping is a synthesis of those sources, not a published syllabus. In the formulas, *s* is the bet as a fraction of the pot. One bet-size slider can teach required equity, MDF, alpha and bluff ratio together, because each is a simple function of *s*.

| # | Unit and key concept | Formula / anchor fact | How the engine generates and grades it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **EV and pot odds**: maximize EV, not win rate | Required equity = call ÷ final pot = s/(2s+1). ½-pot needs 25%; pot-size needs 33% ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/mathematical-misconceptions-in-poker/), [toy games](https://blog.gtowizard.com/how-to-solve-toy-games/)) | Random pot and bet sizes give an exact answer; grade the error in percentage points. This is the existing pot-odds drill |
| 2 | **Equity vs ranges, and calibration** | Equity realization (EQR) = share of pot won ÷ raw equity; raw equity is not EV ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the-10-most-important-concepts-for-new-poker-players/)) | Exact or Monte Carlo equity gives the true value; score slider estimates with Brier score. Extends the equity quiz |
| 3 | **Preflop opening ranges by position** | 6-max, 100bb opening ranges: UTG 16%, CO 24.9%, BTN 40.9%, SB 47.8%; raise, don't limp ([Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/preflop-open-strategy-rfi-explained/)) | Range trainer graded against a licensed or self-solved chart. Note: no sourced 3-bet chart was found |
| 4 | **What GTO is**: unexploitable is not maximum profit | Kuhn poker value: −1/18 for player 1 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuhn_poker)) | CFR solves Kuhn in microseconds; the learner plays the bot and sees results converge |
| 5 | **Toy games**: clairvoyance and AKQ teach indifference | MDF = 1/(1+s); α = s/(1+s); bluff share of a polarized range = s/(2s+1). Unexploitable bluff-catches have EV 0 ([MDF & Alpha](https://blog.gtowizard.com/mdf-alpha/), [toy games](https://blog.gtowizard.com/how-to-solve-toy-games/), [bluff catching](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the-art-of-bluff-catching-2/)) | Learner sets bluff or call frequencies; engine computes the exploitability of that choice in closed form |
| 6 | **Push/fold** at ≤15bb | Push/fold to about 15bb (20bb if weak postflop); 10bb SB shoves 75.2% ([Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/push-fold-tournament-strategy-charts/)) | Existing CFR+ Nash solver; grade push/fold by EV loss in bb at random stack depths |
| 7 | **Postflop structure**: range vs nut advantage, board texture, SPR | Range advantage drives c-bet frequency; nut advantage drives size. Dry QQ6 bets ⅓ pot mostly; wet KJ7 uses 75–125% ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the-mechanics-of-c-bet-sizing/)) | Range-vs-range equity on random flops for "who has the advantage?" questions; river solver for sizing |
| 8 | **Advanced balance**: polarized vs merged ranges, blockers, mixing | Three laws: selfish EV, indifference, fixed strategies ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the-three-laws-of-indifference/)) | River solver; blocker questions computed by card removal; mixed hands graded as equal-EV |
| 9 | **Exploits** | A nit's river raise: GTO says call with ~25.5% equity, but against that player your equity is ~0%, so over-fold ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the_3_biggest_leaks_killing_your_winrate/)) | Lock the villain's strategy in a river solve and show how the hero's best response changes |
| 10 | **ICM and caveats** | Risk-premium example: chip-EV needs 37.5%, ICM needs about 47% ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/icm-basics/)) | ICM push/fold solver; multiway, rake and AI-history lessons as short reading cards |

Build "myth-buster" lessons around the leaks that sources say are most common. These are treating raw equity as EV, using MDF as a reason to call, bluffing into strong ranges, being passive with strong hands, limping, and ignoring ICM ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/mathematical-misconceptions-in-poker/); [GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the_3_biggest_leaks_killing_your_winrate/)). The MDF caveat deserves its own lesson. MDF only makes 0%-equity bluffs break even, and solvers defend below MDF out of position ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/mdf-alpha/)). Under the Law of Fixed Strategies, exact mixing frequencies matter little against a fixed opponent, so beginners should simplify to pure actions ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the-three-laws-of-indifference/)).

## Commercial trainers grade by frequency, leaving clarity as the opening

The market splits three ways. Desktop solvers (PioSOLVER, GTO+) have no real trainer. GTO Wizard runs in the browser and has the deepest trainer. Mobile drill apps (DTO, Postflop+, Preflop+, ICMIZER) are spot-based ([DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/tools/gto-solvers)). GTO Wizard gives each move one of five labels: Best (highest frequency, or chosen by its RNG roll), Correct (some non-zero frequency), Inaccuracy (under 3.5%), Wrong, and Blunder. It also tracks EV loss in bb and as % of pot. It does not publish what counts as "significant" EV loss for a Blunder ([GTO Wizard Help](https://help.gtowizard.com/measure-performance/)). Its trainer offers Simple, Grouped and Standard action sets, plus a Frequency input mode ([GTO Wizard Help](https://help.gtowizard.com/how-to-use-the-trainer/)). Grading by frequency produces real complaints. A Postflop+ reviewer asked why **two equal-EV actions were rated "OK" and "Perfect"**, and another said +EV plays were labeled blunders ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/postflop-gto-poker-trainer/id1488850006)). Adaptivity and gamification are thin. ICMIZER has placement tests and 0–100 ratings, but its mobile Pro tier allows only **5 questions/day** ([ICMIZER](https://icmizer.com/icmizer/sng-coach-features)). Poker Academy, the closest to Duolingo with XP, spaced repetition and weekly plans, had **1 rating** when checked ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1626050814)). Prices run from about $10/month for mobile apps to **$39–279/month** for GTO Wizard ([PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2026/03/gto-wizard-subscription-plans-new-features-pricing-50908.htm)). Forum users say solvers are wrong for beginners ([PokerChipForum](https://www.pokerchipforum.com/threads/feedback-on-poker-training-websites-gto-wizard-or-pokercoaching-com.141059/)).

PokerLab can stand out with **EV-loss-first grading and published thresholds**. Grade by EV lost as a % of pot, treat any action within a small tolerance of the best EV as correct, and print the tolerance in the explanation. Show the solver's EV for every option, so the "why" is concrete. Also include a short "results vs. decisions" lesson. DTO's developer had to explain to a user that calling all-in into the nuts can still be correct play over the long run ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/it/app/dto-mtt/id1458491856)).

## An iPhone can solve rivers instantly, turns in seconds, flops only precomputed

Start with the algorithm. **Discounted CFR (DCFR) with parameters (3/2, 0, 2) "matches or outperforms CFR+ across the board,"** usually by **2–3×**. Plain CFR+ also struggles when some actions are very costly mistakes, such as folding the nuts, which is common in poker trees ([Brown & Sandholm](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1809.04040)). Adding DCFR is a few-line switch on your existing CFR+ code. Sampling methods (MCCFR) and neural methods (Deep CFR) target games too large to traverse fully, which a river or turn is not ([Deep CFR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00164v2)).

For a desktop reference point, the fastest open-source solver solves a full flop to **0.1% pot exploitability in 44 s on 6 desktop threads, using 1.27 GB** (679 MB with 16-bit compression) ([wasm-postflop](https://www.github.com/b-inary/wasm-postflop)). It takes **35.7 s natively on an M2 MacBook Air** ([discussion #24](https://www.github.com/b-inary/postflop-solver/discussions/24)). That solver uses no card abstraction. It merges suit-isomorphic deals and solves the showdown with a sort-and-sweep ([postflop-solver](https://github.com/b-inary/postflop-solver)). GPUs only pay off on large trees. One GPU CFR implementation was **85× slower than C++ on Kuhn poker** ([Kim](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.14778v4)), but on an A100 a restricted river subgame ran at 0.21 ms/iteration on GPU versus 1.70 ms on 8 CPU threads ([GPU-CFR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11923)).

The phone estimates below come from the research notes' own arithmetic, not from published iPhone benchmarks. None exist for any iOS solver, and apps like PocketSolver do not say whether they solve on-device ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pocketsolver/id6479562719?uo=4)).

| Feature | Estimated size and time on an A17/A18 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Toy games (Kuhn, AKQ, clairvoyance, [0,1]) and Leduc (about 9.5k nodes) | Microseconds to well under 1 s | Ship now |
| ICM push/fold, final table | Exact ICM by subset dynamic programming: about 4.6k states for 9 players | Ship; fits the current ~80 ms budget |
| River solver, full ranges, 1–2 bet sizes | Under 1 MB; roughly 0.1–1 s for 1,000 iterations on CPU SIMD (NEON) | **Flagship feature** |
| Turn solver, 1 size per street | About 30–300 MB; roughly 5–60 s, with live strategy updates | Stretch goal; try Metal here |
| Flop solver | 0.7–1.3 GB, minutes; near iOS memory-kill (jetsam) limits | Precompute offline and ship compressed |
| Neural value net (ReBeL-style) | Needs GPU-cluster training ([ReBeL](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.13544)) | Out of scope |

Use native Swift with NEON rather than WebAssembly. WebKit bug reports document WASM pages killed for memory on iOS ([WebKit 256703](https://lists.webkit.org/pipermail/webkit-unassigned/2023-May/1103477.html); [WebKit 255103](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=255103)). Exploitability costs about one best-response pass to compute, so you can show it live in "% of pot," the unit users know ([wasm-postflop](https://www.github.com/b-inary/wasm-postflop)).

## FSRS decides when to review, Elo decides what to serve

The best-supported study techniques are **practice testing and spacing**, both rated "high utility." Rereading and highlighting rate low ([ScienceDaily on Dunlosky 2013](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/01/130110111734.htm)). So make most lessons "choose an action, then get feedback." Interleave easily confused spots, such as dry vs. wet boards or in position vs. out of position. Interleaving gives **g = 0.42** overall and works best when categories look alike ([Brunmair & Richter](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31556629/)). One small study found novice poker players improved most under time pressure, which supports a timed mode ([St. Germain & Tenenbaum](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/decision-making-and-thought-processes-among-poker-players/)).

For scheduling, **FSRS beats SM-2 for 99.6% of users** on a 727M-review Anki benchmark. It uses about 21 weights, and personalized weights beat the defaults in 84% of collections ([Expertium](https://expertium.github.io/Benchmark.html)). Its core formulas are a few exp/pow calls ([Borretti](https://borretti.me/article/implementing-fsrs-in-100-lines)), and a swift-fsrs port exists ([awesome-fsrs](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs)). Duolingo's older half-life regression model, p = 2^(−Δ/h), cut prediction error sharply versus Leitner (MAE 0.128 vs 0.235). In a live test, though, it moved retention only **+0.3%** ([Settles & Meeder](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/settles.acl16.pdf)). Its streak redesign moved day-14 retention **+3.3%** ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)). The lesson is that the UX around the model matters as much as the model.

For picking the next question, use **Elo**: P(correct) = σ(θ − d), with step size K = a/(1+b·n). Serve items with about a 75% predicted success rate ([Pelánek](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/CAE-elo.pdf)). Duolingo's Birdbrain does a similar job, predicting correctness and choosing exercises "at the right difficulty" ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/learning-how-to-help-you-learn-introducing-birdbrain)). Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) gives a clear per-concept "mastery %." Extended BKT **matches deep knowledge tracing on average** with about 200 parameters versus about 250k ([Khajah et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1604.02416)), so a neural model is not justified for a single-user app. One poker-specific adjustment matters. Make each review card a *spot template*, such as "BTN vs BB, monotone flop," with a fresh deal every time. That way the system schedules the concept, not memory of one hand. Map EV loss to FSRS grades: Again for a big loss, Hard for a small one, Good for correct, Easy for correct and fast. Feed exp(−EVloss/τ) into Elo as partial credit, where τ is a tuning constant. Finally, add a calibration game. About an hour of probability training improved forecasters' **Brier scores by 6–11%** ([EA Forum on GJP](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/W94KjunX3hXAtZvXJ/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good)). Equity sliders scored by Brier score, with a reliability chart, apply the same method to poker.

## Rank features by what a reviewer can measure

The ranking below weighs interview impact against solo-developer effort. Impact and effort are judgment calls. The metrics are the evidence.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **On-device river solver** (DCFR, NEON, sort-and-sweep showdown) | Very high / Medium-high | "Solves full-range river to 0.3% pot exploitability in X ms on A17, peak memory Y MB." Check results against published solver output |
| 2 | **Toy-game lab** with animated CFR convergence | High / Low | "Converges to the Kuhn value −1/18 to within 10⁻⁴ in X ms." Plot exploitability vs iterations |
| 3 | **DCFR switch** on the existing push/fold solver | Medium / Low | "Cut iterations to target exploitability by X× vs CFR+" (paper reports 2–3×) |
| 4 | **EV-loss grading engine** with equal-EV tolerance | High / Low | "Generates and grades N question types; mean EV loss per decision fell X% over M reviews," measured on fresh, unseen deals |
| 5 | **Adaptive layer**: FSRS + Elo/BKT per concept | High / Medium | "Next-answer prediction AUC X / log loss Y vs constant baseline Z," on a held-out past-to-future split |
| 6 | **Calibration game** | Medium / Low | "Users' equity Brier score improved X%." Ship a reliability diagram |
| 7 | **ICM push/fold solver** | Medium / Medium | "Final-table ICM Nash solve in X ms" |
| 8 | **Turn solver + Metal experiment** | High / High | "Found the tree size where Metal beats CPU NEON (X× at Y nodes)" |
| 9 | **Compressed precomputed flop library** | Medium / High | "N flops in X MB (16-bit), lookups under Y ms" |

Sequence the work so every step produces a number. Items 2–4 reuse what you have and can ship in weeks. Item 5 becomes credible once you have your own review logs. Item 1 is the headline. Profile it with Instruments, and publish its benchmark in the README, because no one has published one for an iPhone.

## Conclusion

PokerLab's edge is not out-solving GTO Wizard. It is making solver-grade correctness readable to beginners. All the core math comes from the indifference principle, and your engine already computes the ground truth, so the same code that checks answers can also explain them. The adaptive layer turns that into a measurable learning claim, which most poker apps never make. For an Apple hiring manager, the strongest version of this project shows three things: you know when the GPU loses to SIMD on the CPU, you can validate an algorithm against a known closed-form answer, and you evaluate a learning model on held-out data. Two things remain unknown and are worth testing yourself. No one has validated FSRS for decision skills rather than flashcard recall, and the iPhone solve times above are estimates until you measure them.
